# HomeBusinessWatch Residual Rating Algorithm

**Version:** 1.0  
**Last Updated:** March 26, 2026  
**Author:** Paul Hutchings & Mac

---

## Overview

The Residual Rating measures how well a business opportunity supports **building true residual income** — income that continues without ongoing work requirements. This is the single most important factor for evaluating home business opportunities.

Our rating scale is **0.0 to 5.0**, where:
- **5.0** = Exceptional residual income potential
- **4.0** = Strong residual income potential
- **3.0** = Moderate residual income potential
- **2.0** = Limited residual income potential
- **1.0** = Poor residual income potential
- **0.0** = Disqualified (see automatic disqualifiers)

---

## Automatic Disqualifiers (Score = 0.0)

The following result in an **immediate 0.0 rating** and placement in the "High Risk" category:

1. **Promised passive returns** — Company promises daily, weekly, or compounding returns without clear product/service delivery
2. **No real product** — Company has obscure, vague, hard-to-understand, or no actual product that people use
3. **Regulatory shutdown** — Company has been shut down by FTC, state AGs, or equivalent regulators

If any of these apply, skip all other scoring — the company receives 0.0.

## Scoring Factors

### 1. Per-Customer Residual Income (40% weight)

**The single most important factor.** How much do you earn monthly, residually, per customer in your organization?

| Per-Customer Residual | Score |
|----------------------|-------|
| $100+/month | 5.0 |
| $50-99/month | 4.0 |
| $25-49/month | 3.0 |
| $10-24/month | 2.0 |
| $1-9/month | 1.0 |
| $0 or unclear | 0.5 |

**Important: Business Volume (BV) / Points Systems**  
Many companies pay commissions on "Business Volume" or "Points" rather than actual dollars spent. When calculating per-customer residual:
- **Always calculate the REAL dollar amount earned**, not the percentage of BV/points
- If company pays 10% on 50 BV for a $100 product, that's $5 actual — not $10
- Note the BV-to-dollar ratio in the review (e.g., "50 BV per $100 = 50% of retail")
- Companies with 1:1 dollar-to-commission basis score higher on transparency

**Note on Physical vs Digital Products:**  
Physical products inherently have lower per-customer residuals due to manufacturing, shipping, and inventory costs. This is acknowledged but not adjusted — we score on absolute dollars. When comparing opportunities, readers should consider that digital products naturally have higher margin potential.

**Note on One-Time vs Recurring Purchases:**  
Affiliate programs and direct sales with **one-time purchases only** (no recurring orders) inherently lack residual income potential. For these models:
- Cap the Per-Customer Residual score at **2.0 maximum**
- Note in the review: "One-time purchase model — limited residual potential"
- If the company has a mix of one-time and recurring products, weight toward the recurring portion

---

### 2. Residual Penalties (20% weight)

Does the company penalize your residual payout rate if you stop producing — or keep money that should be yours?

**Natural and fair:** Your team volume drops from $10,000 to $5,000, so your 10% commission drops from $1,000 to $500. That's just math — less volume means less commission. No problem.

**Penalty (what we score against):** Your team volume drops to $5,000, you "lose your rank," and now you only get 5% instead of 10% — so you earn $250 instead of $500. *The company kept $250 that should have been your residual.*

**The question:** If your customers keep buying but you stop working, do you keep earning the same percentage on the volume? Or does the company cut your rate and pocket the difference?

| Policy | Score |
|--------|-------|
| No penalties — you keep your rate on whatever volume exists | 5.0 |
| Simple annual renewal fee only | 4.0 |
| Rank/rate drops if team volume falls below threshold | 1.5 |
| Must maintain personal production or enrollment quotas to keep rate | 1.5 |
| Multiple penalty mechanisms combined | 0.5 |

**Key principle:** If you have to keep working to keep what you built, it's not true residual income.

**Note on Monthly Product Requirements:**  
Monthly purchase requirements are standard in product-based businesses and are NOT automatically a con. The con is only when the requirement **exceeds reasonable personal consumption** — forcing affiliates to stockpile inventory or buy more than they would naturally use. A $100/month skincare order you actually use is not a penalty. A $300/month requirement that forces overbuying is.

---

### 3. Company Stability (15% weight)

How likely is the company to be around long-term, protecting your residual income?

| Factor | Score Adjustment |
|--------|-----------------|
| **Longevity** | |
| 10+ years in business | +1.0 |
| Under 10 years | -1.0 |
| **Comp Plan Changes** | |
| Never changed compensation plan | +0.5 |
| Changed comp plan (any change) | -1.0 |
| **Commission History** | |
| Never missed a commission payment | +0.5 |
| Has missed or delayed payments | -1.0 |

**Base score: 3.0**, then apply adjustments (min 0.5, max 5.0)

---

### 4. Product Value (10% weight)

We evaluate two things: Is this a legitimate product people actually use? And is the price reasonable compared to alternatives?

**A. Product Legitimacy** — Is this a real product that solves a real problem or provides a real benefit?

| Indicator | Score |
|-----------|-------|
| Clear, useful product with genuine demand | 5.0 |
| Legitimate product but niche appeal | 3.5 |
| Product exists but questionable utility | 2.0 |
| Vague, unclear, or no real product | 0.5 |

**B. Price Reasonableness** — How does pricing compare to similar products customers could buy elsewhere?

| Pricing | Score |
|---------|-------|
| Competitive with retail alternatives | 5.0 |
| Slight premium (10-30% above retail) | 4.0 |
| Moderate premium (30-50% above retail) | 3.0 |
| Significant premium (50-100% above retail) | 2.0 |
| Extreme premium or no comparable products | 1.0 |

**Final Product Value score:** Average of Legitimacy + Price Reasonableness

---

### 5. Transparency & Simplicity (10% weight)

Can an average person understand how they'll get paid?

| Factor | Score |
|--------|-------|
| Simple, clear comp plan anyone can understand | 5.0 |
| Moderately complex but documented well | 3.5 |
| Complex comp plan with many ranks/bonuses | 2.0 |
| Very complex, difficult to calculate earnings | 1.0 |
| Comp plan hidden or not publicly available | 0.5 |

**Income Disclosure Statement:**
- Published IDS: No penalty
- Hidden/No IDS: -1.0 (compliance risk = shutdown risk = residual income risk)

---

### 6. Policies & Procedures (5% weight)

Does the company have loopholes that can strip your residual income?

| Factor | Score |
|--------|-------|
| Clean, fair P&P that protect affiliate income | 5.0 |
| Standard P&P with normal compliance requirements | 3.5 |
| P&P with some concerning clauses | 2.0 |
| P&P with multiple pitfalls that could cost residual income | 1.0 |
| Predatory P&P designed to strip income | 0.5 |

**Examples of pitfalls:** Termination for minor violations, arbitrary volume lapses, "use it or lose it" clauses, company ability to change terms without notice.

---

### 7. Startup Costs (0% weight — noted only)

Startup costs don't affect the residual rating but are documented for accessibility:

| Startup Cost | Assessment |
|-------------|------------|
| Free or under $100 | Highly accessible |
| $100-500 | Accessible |
| $500-1,000 | Reasonable |
| $1,000-1,500 | Borderline |
| $1,500-2,000+ | Danger zone — high-ticket risk |

**Note:** High startup costs don't reduce residual income potential, but they increase participant risk. The 80/20 rule applies — most people won't recoup high-ticket investments.

---

### 8. Regulatory History (0% weight — noted only)

Regulatory actions are documented but don't affect the score unless they resulted in material harm:

| Regulatory Status | Treatment |
|------------------|-----------|
| Clean regulatory history | Note: No regulatory actions |
| FTC/AG warning or investigation (company continues) | Note on page, no score impact |
| Settlement reached, company complied and continues | Note on page, no score impact |
| Company shut down or materially damaged | Automatic 0.0 (disqualified) |

---

## Final Score Calculation

```
Final Score = (Per-Customer Residual × 0.40)
            + (Maintenance Requirements × 0.20)
            + (Company Stability × 0.15)
            + (Product Value × 0.10)
            + (Transparency × 0.10)
            + (Policies & Procedures × 0.05)
```

Round to one decimal place.

## Application Notes

1. **When data is unavailable:** Use conservative estimates and note "unverified" in the review
2. **When company has multiple products:** Calculate per-customer residual for full product suite
3. **When physical vs digital:** Score on absolute dollars, note the context in review text
4. **When regulatory action exists:** Always document in the review, only disqualify if shutdown/material damage

## Example Scoring

**Example: Home Business Academy**
- Per-Customer Residual: $128/mo → 5.0 × 0.40 = 2.00
- Maintenance: None → 5.0 × 0.20 = 1.00
- Stability: 10+ years, never changed comp plan, never missed payment → 5.0 × 0.15 = 0.75
- Product Value: Digital tools with real utility → 4.5 × 0.10 = 0.45
- Transparency: Simple one-tier, clear documentation → 5.0 × 0.10 = 0.50
- P&P: Clean, affiliate-friendly → 5.0 × 0.05 = 0.25
- **Final Score: 4.95 → 5.0**

**Example: Traditional MLM (Physical Products)**
- Per-Customer Residual: $10/mo → 2.0 × 0.40 = 0.80
- Maintenance: Monthly volume requirements → 2.0 × 0.20 = 0.40
- Stability: 30 years, but changed comp plan twice → 2.5 × 0.15 = 0.375
- Product Value: Strong retail base → 4.5 × 0.10 = 0.45
- Transparency: Complex multi-tier → 2.0 × 0.10 = 0.20
- P&P: Standard with some volume lapses → 3.0 × 0.05 = 0.15
- **Final Score: 2.375 → 2.4**
